# Kaun Hai? authoring notes

This document contains spoilers.

## Premise

A repair technician is alone in the administrative office of an old temple
trust during a monsoon power cut. At 2:13 a.m., a dead UPS reactivates the
complaint terminal and reopens complaint 108/1998. The complaint connects
altered flood-relief donation receipts, the disappearance of night clerk
Devendra Sahu, a copied signature, and a closure ordered without an
investigation.

The supernatural pressure is attached to an unresolved record and the people
erased by it. The temple and its worshippers are not the target of the story;
the wrongdoing belongs to the administrative office.

## Tone and voice

The target balance is quiet horror with occasional dry bureaucratic comedy.
The terminal remains formal and terse rather than becoming a conversational
villain. Comedy comes from labels, forms, inherited procedures, and the
institutional instinct to assign responsibility before discovering truth.

Hindi is limited to the terminal's central question, “Kaun hai wahan?”, where
it sounds natural and carries narrative weight. Strong scenes avoid jokes,
generic fear reactions, gore, glitch text, and exaggerated supernatural
description.

## Motifs

The story reuses a controlled set of details:

- the UPS relay marks escalation and repeats the crow's rhythm;
- damp receipt paper carries records that official systems discarded;
- the carbon-copy register preserves physical evidence;
- the brass corridor bell changes from office equipment to witness procedure;
- cold tea and the recently occupied chair imply an ongoing night shift;
- monsoon water observes boundaries the office staff ignored;
- the crow taps 1-0-8 and draws attention to a contradictory warning;
- open, pending, and resolved statuses frame truth as an administrative choice.

## Route structure

The 23-node graph contains 19 active nodes and four ending nodes.

- The opening exposes the impossible boot, complaint 108, the bell, the crow,
  and the first question.
- The complaint route identifies Devendra and Kamla but initially hides the
  closing officer.
- The register route recovers the carbon impression and the initials J.N.B.
- The crow route reveals S. Rao's unreliable procedure and contains an
  optional two-node cycle with several exits.
- The corridor route explains the service bell and permits the deliberate
  fourth-bell sequence.
- The record room joins the paper and terminal evidence before the player
  chooses whether to testify, accept an appointment, or use an override.
- The truthful route requires a timestamp comparison, a copied-signature
  finding, and a declaration limited to what the present-day witness can
  actually prove.

Branches converge only where the terminal displays the necessary audit trail,
so later prose does not pretend that untracked evidence was collected.

## Ending intent

- `complaint-closed`: The strongest outcome. The player distinguishes witnessed
  facts from inference, records the falsified closure honestly, and causes a
  real inquiry to begin.
- `permanent-night-shift`: Responsibility is mistaken for truth. Signing the
  wrong field lets the system fill a vacancy that has existed since 1998.
- `unknown-administrator`: Force-closing the complaint confirms authority for
  the unnamed presence already using copied signatures.
- `fourth-bell`: The player knowingly tests a disputed warning. The result
  collapses witness, statement, and date without explaining the corridor away.

## Story Document v1 constraints

The format has no inventory, flags, conditional text, or hidden state.
Continuity therefore lives in direct branch structure. The carbon impression
is read and immediately acted upon; the bell count advances through a dedicated
node; and shared resolution scenes contain evidence displayed at that moment.

The current format cannot remember which fragments a player discovered before
returning to a shared node. A future condition system could vary the final
terminal summary, acknowledge accumulated evidence, or make the crow route
alter later choices. Inventory could support physically carrying the brass key
or original complaint, but this episode deliberately makes neither a hidden
requirement.

## Clichés deliberately avoided

The prose avoids bodily fear shorthand, generic sinister presences, repeated
“suddenly” openings, excessive ellipses, corruption spam, dream or simulation
reveals, gore, cartoonish crow behavior, and jokes during the strongest horror
beats. It also avoids treating faith, worship, priests, or devotees as the
source of comedy or threat.
